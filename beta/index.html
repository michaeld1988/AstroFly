Tip: prefer TIFF or PNG. JPEG compression creates artifacts in dark areas that can affect the depth map and the nebula-color masks.

For single moon shots, no star mask needed: AstroFly detects the lunar disk and replaces the brightness depth map with a true sphere, so the moon reads as round during camera moves. Generated background stars (Stars tab) stay behind the disk. Works best with a bright, (nearly) full moon against dark sky.

Flight plan (waypoints)

Chain camera moves: zoom into the center, glide to a detail, pull back out. Each waypoint stores a position, a zoom level, travel seconds and an easing. The camera visits them in order; the video length adapts automatically. Rotation and sway keep working on top.

Steer the view with the pad in the preview: arrows pan, + and - zoom, the arcs rotate. When the framing is right, press the center button (or the one below) to save it as a waypoint, then steer on to the next object. Resetting the view never deletes waypoints. Depth map

Computed automatically from the brightness of the starless image: bright nebula regions appear "closer" to the camera.

The custom map must match the starless image exactly (same framing; white = near, black = far). Export the automatic map, refine it in any editor, load it back - smoothing and invert still apply, and mirroring follows the image automatically.

Mirroring

If your processed image is mirrored compared to the real sky (check against Stellarium or Aladin), flip it here - starless and star mask flip together, labels and the Gaia match follow automatically. Re-run the Gaia match afterwards.

Flight

The camera glides across the image at constant zoom – near regions and stars drift past faster (parallax). Speed sets the travel distance, a click sets the path center. Increase zoom for a longer travel range.

Strength of the parallax – how clearly near and far regions move at different speeds.

Zoom target: Click the preview to choose where the camera flies to. Double-click = back to center. Target: image center

Galaxy spin

Galaxy spin: rotates only the region around the spin center – with a soft falloff at the edge, so the rest of the image stays untouched. Foreground stars do not rotate.

Stars inside the spin area follow the galaxy's rotation and stay locked to its structure. Note: foreground stars that just happen to sit in front of the galaxy will rotate too – toggle as it suits your image.

Star field

How strongly the stars move relative to the nebula – higher values = more depth impression.

The brightest detected stars are rendered with their actual pixels from the star mask - diffraction spikes, halos and exact colors included - instead of an idealized glow sprite. Faint stars stay procedural (they are only a few pixels anyway). During strong motion streaks a star falls back to the procedural sprite.

Layers, colors & extra stars

Adds synthetic stars with realistic brightness and star colors – ideal for lateral flights or sparse star masks. The dice button rolls a new arrangement.

Nebula occlusionExperimental

Stars whose depth places them BEHIND the nebula are dimmed by the nebula's density (bright, dense regions swallow more starlight than thin wisps) – during a flyby, stars visibly disappear behind clouds and re-emerge. Stars in front of the nebula are unaffected. 0 = off.

Effects & look

Color & detail

Nebula colors (Hα · OIII · SII)

Adjusts the typical emission-line colors selectively: red (Hα), teal (OIII) and golden (SII) areas of the nebula. Works on the hue, so it fits RGB and narrowband palettes alike – gray areas and the stars stay untouched.

Real data (Gaia & SIMBAD)

Real star distances (Gaia): load a plate-solved version of your image (FITS from ASTAP, PixInsight or Siril, or a .wcs file from astrometry.net) – AstroFly then matches your detected stars against the ESA Gaia catalog and assigns each one its real measured distance. The 3D layers follow the actual depth of the sky. Requires an internet connection for the catalog query; only sky coordinates are transferred, never your image.

…

Shows only stars with a real Gaia distance, at their real depth – detected stars without a catalog match and generated stars are hidden. Unlocks at a match rate of 75 % or higher, so the star field doesn't thin out too much.

Matched stars get their physically correct hue from the measured Gaia color index (blue-white = hot, orange-red = cool) – brightness still comes from your photo. Great for narrowband images, where star colors are never real. Unmatched stars keep their photo color.

Matched stars drift along their real measured trajectories (Gaia proper motion) over the course of the video – e.g. 200,000 years of stellar motion compressed into your clip. 0 = off.

Physically, a galaxy or nebula sits far behind every foreground star. This option treats the image as one rigid, distant object: it barely reacts to the flight while all stars pass in front – the most scientifically accurate 3D behavior. The depth-map parallax inside the image is disabled while active.

Always on Stars sitting on dense nebula regions automatically adopt the nebula's depth and motion – they stay inside the nebula during the 3D flight instead of drifting in front of it (like the Wolf-Rayet stars in the Lion Nebula). After a Gaia match, stars clearly in front of or behind the nebula stay free.

Objects in the field

Object info & field labels: with a plate-solve solution loaded, AstroFly can identify the objects in your image via the SIMBAD database (CDS) and render an info card (distance, size, stars, age for popular objects) plus labeled markers into the video. Only sky coordinates are transferred.

Aspect ratio

The crop is automatically tightened so rotation, tilt and drift can never push it past the image edges. If disabled you get a wider framing, but the borders are mirrored there and the stars end before the frame edge.
